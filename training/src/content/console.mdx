You've driven the gateway from the CLI. The same gateway has a web UI: the **OpenShell Console** ([ai-openshell/console](https://github.com/ai-openshell/console)), deployed on its own host. It isn't a separate system: it forwards your Keycloak token to the same gateway, so it sees and does exactly what *you* may see and do.

<Section n="1" title="Sign in to the OpenShell Console">

The Console is the gateway in a browser. Sign in as `demo`, the platform admin, with Keycloak.

<Step n="1" title="Get the demo users' passwords">

The passwords are generated per box:

```bash
grep -E 'KC_(DEMO|LEARNER)_PASSWORD' .sso-secrets.env
```

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Sign in as demo">

Use the **OpenShell Console ↗** link in the site header, or open `https://console-<brevid>.<domain>`. You're sent to Keycloak; sign in as **`demo`**.

The top bar shows **Gateway status: Healthy** and **Role: Platform admin**: the gateway read `openshell-admin` from your token.

<Fig id="architecture" n="3.1" caption="The Console signs you in with Keycloak and calls the same gateway as your CLI." />

</Step>

</Section>

<Section n="2" title="Find your way around the console">

- **Workspaces**: tenants, their members, sandboxes, providers and templates ([next lesson](/learn/access-control)).
- **Sandboxes**: every sandbox you may see, across workspaces, with status and image.
- **Proposals**: the inbox of policy changes waiting for review (below).
- **Provider profiles** and **Global policy**: platform-wide settings, admins only.

</Section>

<Section n="3" title="Look at one sandbox from every tab">

Open `agent-0` and match each tab to the CLI command you already know.

<Step n="1" title="Open agent-0's page">

Open **Sandboxes → agent-0**. The tabs map one-to-one to what you did in the shell:

| Tab | What it shows | The CLI equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Details | id, image, command, limits, attached providers, active policy version | `openshell sandbox get agent-0` |
| Logs | gateway + sandbox logs with the OCSF decisions, filterable by level and source | `openshell logs agent-0` |
| Terminal | a shell inside the workload pod, in your browser | `openshell sandbox connect agent-0` |
| Providers | attach and detach providers | `openshell sandbox provider attach/detach` |
| Policy | static part (filesystem, Landlock, process) and the editable network rules, with revisions | `openshell policy get / update / list` |
| Proposals | rules suggested from denied traffic | (below) |
| Services | HTTP services exposed from the sandbox | `openshell service expose` |
| Settings | per-sandbox settings, policy source and hash | `openshell settings get agent-0` |
| Files | upload and download | `openshell sandbox upload / download` |

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Edit the policy from the console">

Open **Policy**: you'll see `agent-0`'s baseline and `_provider_litellm`, merged in from the provider. Add or remove an endpoint here and run `openshell policy list agent-0` in the Cluster shell: a new revision, the same one.

</Step>

</Section>

<Section n="4" title="Proposals: denied traffic becomes a rule to review">

When an agent keeps hitting a denied destination, the gateway analyses the denials and drafts a rule: a **proposal**, pending until a person decides.

<Step n="1" title="Make some denied traffic">

From `agent-0`, try GitHub's API five times. Its policy has no rule for it, so each attempt is denied:

```bash
openshell sandbox exec -n agent-0 -- sh -c 'for i in 1 2 3 4 5; do curl -s -m 5 -o /dev/null https://api.github.com/zen; done; echo done'
```

</Step>

<Step n="2" title="Read the proposal from the CLI">

**`openshell rule get`** lists a sandbox's proposed network rules (give it a few seconds after the denials):

```bash
openshell rule get agent-0 | head -13
```
<Output summary="A pending rule, the binary that asked, a confidence score, and how often it was denied.">
<ExpectedOutput>
{`Network Rules:  (version 3, 3 chunks)

  Chunk: a677be68-78df-4e1c-b468-9d2a37dfea89
  Status: pending
  Rule: allow_api_github_com_443
  Binary: /usr/bin/curl
  Confidence: 75%
  Rationale: Allow curl to connect to api.github.com:443 (HTTPS).
  Prover: prover: no new findings
  Candidate: 22279106d24e
  Endpoints: api.github.com:443 [L4]
  Binaries: /usr/bin/curl
  Hits: 5 (first seen 2026-11-13 12:07:54, last seen 2026-11-13 12:07:54)`}
</ExpectedOutput>
</Output>

</Step>

<Step n="3" title="Decide, in the console">

Open **Proposals** for `agent-0` in the console: the same pending rule. Nothing changes until a person decides. **Approve** turns it into a new policy revision; **Reject** records why. Here the right call is *reject*: `agent-0` has no business calling GitHub. From the CLI it's `openshell rule reject agent-0 <chunk id>`. That's how a policy grows from real behaviour without ever loosening silently.

</Step>

</Section>

<Callout type="note" title="CLI and console are one control plane">
Create a sandbox in the console, then run <code>openshell sandbox list</code> here: it's there. The console has no privileges of its own; sign in as <code>learner</code> and it shows only what <code>learner</code> may see.
</Callout>

## What you learned

<Learned next="/learn/access-control" nextLabel="Users, roles & workspaces">

- **The Console is the gateway in a browser.** It signs you in with Keycloak and forwards your token; it has no privileges of its own.
- **Every tab is a CLI command you know**: sandboxes, logs, the terminal, policy revisions.
- **Denied traffic becomes a proposal**: a pending rule that changes nothing until a person approves or rejects it.
