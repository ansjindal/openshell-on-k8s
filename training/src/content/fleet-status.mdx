<Section n="1" title="Read each agent's card">

Each card is one agent, read live from the cluster:

- **Status**: `Ready` means the gateway has the sandbox up and its policy loaded.
- **May reach**: the hosts in its *effective* policy (`openshell policy get <agent> --full`). Every agent reaches LiteLLM (through the provider) and the skill repository; the specialists also reach the **sre-tools MCP** server.
- **May call**: the tool names its MCP rule allows:
  - `logs` may call `loki_query`,
  - `metrics` may call `prometheus_query`,
  - `traces` may call `tempo_search`,
  - `events` may call `cluster_events` and `pod_status`,
  - `analyst` has **no tools**: it only concludes.
- **ROLE.md**: the role the agent was given, with its path in the sandbox.

There's no honor system here. If the `metrics` agent tried `loki_query`, its supervisor would refuse the call, and you'd see the denial in its audit log, with the tool's name and the rule. No agent may call `scale_deployment`.

</Section>

<Callout type="rh" title="This is the answer to 'can I trust a fleet of agents?'">
Not "trust" as a vibe — trust as something you <strong>read off a page</strong>. Every tool each agent may call and every place it can reach is explicit, per-agent, and auditable. Add a tenth agent and the picture stays just as legible.
</Callout>
