A quick look back at Module 2 before the capstone: customizing agents, then operating them.

## What you learned

- You can build your own provider, attach it to a running sandbox, rotate its token and detach it, all without a restart.
- An agent's persona is plain Markdown in its workspace (`IDENTITY.md`, `SOUL.md`, `BOOTSTRAP.md`), re-read each session.
- Skills are governed three ways: the registry they come from, whether they are enabled, and what they can reach.
- `./scripts/fleet task` hands a prompt to one agent or the whole fleet, each working in its own sandbox.
- Every supervisor decision (connections, HTTP requests, refused DNS names, policy loads) is written as an OCSF event you can read with `openshell logs`.
- Alloy ships the supervisors' logs to Loki, so the same events for every sandbox are queryable with LogQL and in Grafana Explore; agent OpenTelemetry spans go to Tempo.
- The OpenShell Console forwards your Keycloak token to the same gateway as the CLI: it has no privileges of its own.
- Proposals turn denied traffic into draft rules; nothing changes until a person approves or rejects them.
- A user's Keycloak realm role (`openshell-admin` or `openshell-user`) and their workspace memberships decide what they can see and do.
- Workspaces are tenants with their own sandboxes, providers and members; members are added by their OIDC subject.
